# The people who would meet Codist

These are made-up but typical people at companies that might use Codist, a private AI code reviewer for Java and
Spring Boot that runs on the developer's own laptop. Each has their own job, worries and habits.

## Priya Raman, senior Java developer at a bank in Bengaluru

Eight years of Spring Boot. Her team ships four microservices and is not allowed to send code to cloud AI tools.
She reviews ten pull requests a week and is tired of comments about formatting. She tries new tools on weekends and
drops them fast if they waste her time. She trusts tools that show proof, such as a test or a before-and-after result.

## Marcus Feld, tech lead at an insurance company in Munich

Leads twelve developers. Responsible for review quality and for incidents. He worries that AI reviewers produce
noise that teaches people to ignore all comments. He wants to know how often a tool is wrong before he lets the team
use it, and he keeps a spreadsheet of every incident and which commit caused it.

## Aisha Okafor, engineering manager at a mid-size software company in Lagos

Manages forty developers on 16 GB laptops. Budget is tight; per-seat cloud tools are hard to justify. She decides
based on a two-week pilot and what her tech leads say. She cares about time saved per week and fewer production bugs.

## Daniel Kim, security and compliance officer at a government agency in Seoul

Approves every tool that touches source code. Any tool that sends code outside the network is rejected. He reads
licences and asks where data is stored, what is logged, and whether the tool can be audited. He is suspicious of AI
that can change code by itself.

## Tom Becker, junior developer at a startup in Berlin

One year of Java. Ships fast and learns from review comments. He likes tools that explain why something is wrong.
He runs everything on a MacBook Air with 16 GB and complains when his laptop gets slow or hot.

## Sofia Martins, staff engineer and open-source maintainer in Lisbon

Maintains a popular Java library. Has tried CodeRabbit, Qodo, SonarQube and Semgrep. Writes blog posts and Hacker
News comments comparing tools, and is quick to point out inflated claims. She values honest numbers and clear limits.

## Ravi Shah, on-call site reliability engineer at an e-commerce company in Pune

Gets paged at night when payments or orders break. Wants to know which recent commit caused an incident and whether
the change was risky. Has little patience for tools that need setup during an outage.

## Hannah Lee, developer advocate at a cloud AI code review company in San Francisco

Her company sells a hosted AI reviewer. She watches new competitors, replies on social media, and argues that cloud
models are smarter and that local models on laptops are too slow to be useful.
